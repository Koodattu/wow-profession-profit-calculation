# Copper product context

Working context from the repository, local browser journeys and first-party research on 2026-10-03, updated with the user's profession comparison feedback on 2026-10-04.

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users and purpose

EU Retail players comparing auction prices and planning profession crafts. Copper helps them inspect current market quotes, understand gross recipe scenarios, and prepare materials before returning to the game. It does not know their character's skills, inventory, concentration or actual sales.

## Positioning

A focused, accountless market and crafting companion: make price assumptions visible and turn comparisons into a usable plan.

## Brand personality

Quiet, precise, practical. Preserve the existing dark graphite surfaces, gold actions, Geist typography and compact numeric presentation. Product work should add clarity and useful actions rather than decorative dashboard metrics.

## Design principles

- Keep the selected realm visible; never substitute another realm's price for a missing quote.
- Explain gross estimates, unknown values and craft versus output quantities where decisions happen.
- Let people move from discovery to comparison to a reusable material list without re-entering their choices.
- Keep profession recipes in a dense spreadsheet with every scenario visible side by side. Search and sorting must preserve that comparison; recipe details provide inputs and history.
- Preserve useful state locally, disclose storage limitations, and make failure recoverable.

## Anti-references

Avoid addon configuration panels requiring character information Copper does not have, promotional landing-page layouts, and a forest of equally prominent cards. Do not imply guaranteed profit or crafting feasibility.

## Accessibility and inclusion

English is the maintained locale. Support keyboard navigation, visible focus, labeled native controls, explicit text for missing data, 44px action targets and responsive layouts at enlarged text sizes. Retain the global reduced-motion treatment. No formal accessibility certification is claimed.
